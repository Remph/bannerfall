The first feature release since 1.2. Worlds can now be won: by holding most of the map, by holding the banner keeps, or by having the most points on an end date. The market trades with the village itself, NPC lords play smarter and farm less, the map draws troop movements as lines, and the whole interface got a new look: a green accent (gold in dark mode), a drawn village, slimmer phone screens and desktop pages that use the full width. It builds on v1.2.3; existing worlds keep running and update their database by themselves (migrations V31 to V33).

**Heads-up for running worlds:** world end is on after the update, in the default mode **Domination** (60 % of all villages for 48 hours). A world where one side already holds that much would end two days after the update. Look at the victory card above the ranking after the start, and set the victory mode to "Host ends it" if the world shouldn't end that way (see *For hosts*).

## What is new

**World end and victory**
- The host picks how a world is won (admin page, tab World, card "Victory"):
  - **Domination** (default): a side that holds 60 % of all villages on the map (barbarian villages count in the total) and is alone ahead wins once it has held that for 48 hours in a row
  - **Banner keeps:** five strong barbarian villages (wall 10, Town Hall, mines, warehouse and farm at 10, 1,000 spearmen, 1,000 swordsmen and 100 heavy cavalry) stand in a ring around the middle of the map. A side that holds 51 % of them, rounded up (3 of 5), and is alone ahead wins after 48 hours. Keeps are drawn as forts with a gold flag; otherwise they are ordinary barbarian villages that grow, can be farmed and conquered, and stay keeps for the new owner
  - **Host ends it:** nobody wins by conquest
- In every mode the host can set an **end date**: when it comes, the side with the most points wins (then the most villages). "End now" ends the world at once
- A side is a guild with all its members while guilds are on, otherwise a single player; players without a guild count alone and NPC lords take part. Share, hold time, number of keeps and their share can be set, also in a running world; the condition is checked every five real minutes
- The **victory card** above the ranking leads with the state ("Your guild holds 3 of 5 keeps", "Wins in 1:32:04") and the rule in one sentence, the rest behind "Rules"; on phones it is one line that opens. While a side meets the condition, a strip under the top bar shows it on every page, and every player gets a notification when a side starts the clock, takes it over or loses it
- When a world ends it **freezes**: nothing is produced, nothing moves, timers read "stopped". Reports, messages, chat, the simulator and the ranking stay open, every player sees an end screen with the winner and their own numbers once, and the card turns into the result. The host can reopen the world, and it picks up exactly where it stopped

**Market**
- **Trade with the market itself** (new tab "Exchange"): get one resource for 1.5 of another, at once and without merchants. Each village can receive up to 1,000 × market level × world speed of each resource per day (what it pays doesn't count); the day starts at midnight, Berlin time. A trade that doesn't fit into the warehouse is refused, not cut down. The host sets the rate (1 to 10) or switches it off
- The five market tabs fit a phone

**Farm list**
- **"Send all" no longer stops** at the first entry the troops at home don't cover: it skips it ("not enough troops at home") and goes on to the end of the list. The summary says how many were sent, skipped and not sent. "Send banner chances" works the same way, and an entry it can't send doesn't use up the chance of that player's other villages
- The armed button turns red with a bar over its four seconds; the run's result shows as a chip, sent rows get a check. The banner rules sit behind an (i), the tag reads "Banner from 16:44", and "No banner chance open" replaces an empty button
- "Edit" and "Remove" moved into a "…" menu

**Map**
- **Movement lines** (new switch, off by default, remembered per device): every attack, support and return as a line from start to target, solid behind the troops and dotted ahead, with small arrows. An attack on you is a wide red dashed line on top; it shows the time left but not where the troops are, since that would give away the slowest unit. Tapping a line opens a panel with arrival, distance left, the troops (not for other players' attacks), "Cancel" while that is still possible and links to the start, the target and the Rally Point. Your own troops that have just left fold into one badge at the village
- A village whose **banner chance you already used** shows a faded banner, and its panel says when the chance is back
- Names at medium zoom (your villages by name, other villages by owner), a name plate under the selected village, numbers on the badges in small bubbles, and a drawn landscape of woods, hills and rivers that is the same for every player and changes nothing in the game
- Phones and tablets open both switches and the legend from one "Map layers" button; on desktops the map has one side column with the selected village on top
- The village panel has one lead button (open your village, or attack) and one beside it (support, or farm a barbarian village); the rest are quiet icons

**Village**
- The buildings card opens on a **drawn village**: every building inside a palisade, the mines and the hiding place outside. Buildings grow with their level, the one being built stands in scaffolding with its progress, and each one is a link. "Picture | List" switches back to the list. In dark mode it is an evening village with lit windows
- Village names follow the world's language: "Ben's village" or, in a German world, "Dorf von Ben". The village switcher lists the village held longest first and tags it "Main village"; a new login opens it
- Waiting build orders are one slim line each, "Change order" shows the arrows to move them, and finished orders turn green with a check before they fold away

**Rally Point and reports**
- On phones the Rally Point shows one part at a time: *Send · On the way · Troops*. It opens on *On the way* while an attack is coming. Every own movement and every incoming support has a thin bar filling from departure to arrival; other players' attacks keep their start hidden and link to the attacker on the map. Incoming movements come before outgoing ones
- Reports have filters with counts (All, Attacks, Defence, Scouting, Support, Trade), a heading per day and a line of facts under every battle: loot, losses, and "full haul" when the troops carried all they could. "Select" turns the list into checkboxes to delete several reports at once
- On desktops, reports and messages show the list and the open report side by side; from 1,600 px the open chat docks as a column on the right

**NPC lords**
- They count the loyalty a noble attack will meet on arrival and a scouted Oath banner, wait with revenge until their whole army could win, keep supports at home while an attack is coming and leave supports in a freshly conquered village
- They **farm less**: a lord stops farming while its loot of the last 24 hours is at least a share of its own daily production (easy 0.25, normal 0.5, hard 1.0), and on easy and normal it sends no farm runs at night (in its quiet hours). While it doesn't farm, it doesn't scout farm targets either
- They build a market early (level 2 after Town Hall 5, level 5 after farm and warehouse 10) and trade with it when exactly one resource is missing for the next building or noble

**Look and feel**
- Green accent (gold in dark mode) instead of brick red; red stays for attacks, losses, deleting and warnings
- One back link to the village overview above every sub-page, settings as one list, calm grey disabled buttons, neutral level badges
- Small motion: sliding switches, sheets that slide away, resources that count up after loot or trades, a stamp on the report verdict, smooth map zoom. Everything stands still with "reduced motion"
- Desktop pages use the full width: troops and training side by side in the village, two columns in the farm list, guild, Smithy and achievements, the ranking next to the victory card
- Pages load the first time they are opened (the first download is about 30 % smaller), and a tab from before an update reloads itself
- Percentages read "10%" in English and "10 %" in German, with one decimal where needed. German names are the same everywhere: "Sammelplatz", "Marktplatz", "Rathaus", "Kavallerie"

**Balancing**
- The **axeman carries 15** instead of 10. It applies at once, also to attacks already on the way

## For hosts

- **Three database migrations**, run by Flyway on the first start:
  - V31: the market trade rate (existing worlds get 1.5, so trading with the market is on) and a table of the day's trades
  - V32: the victory settings (existing worlds get Domination 60 % / 48 hours, no end date, no keeps), the keep flag on villages and the tables for the result
  - V33: the village name language (existing worlds stay English, nothing is renamed) and since when each village has its owner, filled in from the conquest reports or the founding date. That date sorts the village switcher
- **New world settings** (admin page, `bannerfall-admin world set …`), all changeable in a running world: `market-exchange-ratio` (1.5; 0 = off, else 1 to 10), `victory-mode` (`DOMINATION`, `KEEPS` or `NONE`), `domination-percent` (60), `victory-hold-hours` (48), `keep-count` (5, 1 to 12), `keep-percent` (51), `village-name-language` (`EN` or `DE`). Switching to keeps places them at the next check. Switching the name language renames, once, every village that still carries a default name; names players chose stay
- **New admin commands:** `bannerfall-admin world end --at "2026-11-07 20:00"` (Berlin time, or ISO with `Z`), `--now` or `--clear`, and `bannerfall-admin world reopen` (takes the end back and clears the end date)
- **NPC lords:** the difficulties have two new values, the farm budget and the night rest (easy 0.25 with rest, normal 0.5 with rest, hard 1.0 without). A CUSTOM difficulty saved before 1.3 has neither until you set them on the admin page. The lord templates gained the two market steps; lords pick everything up on their next round
- **Pinned images:** `compose.prod.yaml` now names `postgres:18.6` and `caddy:2.11.7`, and the Dockerfile pins its Node and Temurin base images. `docker compose up -d` pulls the two if the server doesn't have them yet and restarts the database and Caddy once; it is the same Postgres major version, so nothing is dumped or restored
- New server setting `bannerfall.market.day-zone` (default `Europe/Berlin`): when the market's daily amounts start again. No new environment variables, no changes to the `Caddyfile` or `ops/`
- API: `POST /api/villages/{id}/market/exchange`; `GET /api/world/victory`; `GET /api/map/movement-lines`; `POST /api/reports/delete` (1 to 100 ids); `PUT /api/admin/world-end`, `POST /api/admin/world-end/now` and `/reopen`. The report list takes `filter` and carries `counts`; the world, the map, the market and the admin settings carry the new fields; `GET /api/world/status` can answer `phase: ENDED`; the send-all answer no longer has `stop` and `stoppedAt`. In an ended world every game action answers `409 WORLD_ENDED`
- The web app's API types are checked against a snapshot of the server's OpenAPI file (`pnpm check:api`); `pnpm gen:api` no longer needs a running server

## Upgrading a running world (from v1.2.3)

1. Announce the update (`bannerfall-admin broadcast "Update in 5 minutes"`)
2. Take a backup (`ops/bannerfall-backup`) and check that the file is there
3. `git fetch --tags origin && git checkout v1.3.0`, then either pull the image below or build it yourself with `--build-arg VERSION=1.3.0`; set `BANNERFALL_IMAGE` in `.env` to it and start it (`docker compose -f compose.prod.yaml up -d`). This also brings the pinned Postgres and Caddy images
4. Check the log for the migrations (schema version 33), then `bannerfall-admin status` (version 1.3.0) and `bannerfall-admin check`
5. Look at the victory card above the ranking and decide on the victory mode; for a German world set `village-name-language DE`

Going back to 1.2.3 means restoring the backup from step 2 and starting the old image: the migrations can't be undone, and play since the update is lost.
