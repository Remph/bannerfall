**2.0 gives Bannerfall a new look from the ground up, one list of everything that is running, and signed releases.** Every page now lies on the land your village stands on, with the sky above it. A "Now" list shows builds, training, research, marches and merchants in one place. The rules of the game are unchanged: existing worlds keep running and update their database by themselves (one small migration, V67). The release builds on 1.7.1.

**Heads-up for running worlds:** this is a major version, so `ops/bannerfall-auto-update` will not install it by itself; move to 2.0.0 by hand, after reading these notes. **The first update to 2.0.0 is the one time you should check the release's signature yourself** (see *Signed releases* below), because the update script of 1.7.1 does not check signatures yet. Players see a new look the next time they load the game; their colour choice moves along (see below).

## What Bannerfall is

A browser strategy game for a small group of friends, in German and English, built for phones first and just as usable on a desktop. One world runs for weeks or for good:

- **Villages:** 16 buildings, from the Town Hall to the Banner Hall, a village picture that grows with them, notes and tags per village, demolishing and automatic building plans
- **Troops:** spearmen, swordsmen, axemen, scouts, light and heavy cavalry, rams, catapults, nobles, the paladin, the banner thief and, optionally, archers and mounted archers; unit levels researched in the Smithy; recruit templates; dismissing units
- **War:** attacks, supports, attack trains, scouting, a wall, the night bonus, loyalty and conquest, morale, an attack simulator and detailed battle reports to share
- **Banners:** found in battle, flown from the Banner Hall, fused, stolen by banner thieves and carried as the paladin's standards
- **Economy:** resources and storage, a market with offers and exchange, merchants between villages, a farm list with templates and "Send all"
- **Players together:** guilds with chat, a political map, enemy marks and an optional guild banner; shared reports; private messages; world chat; achievements; statistics with ranking tabs and charts over time
- **NPC lords and barbarians:** computer lords that build, farm, scout, conquer, form guilds, fly banners and learn from players, so a world with only a few people still has opponents; barbarian villages to farm, with walls
- **How a world ends:** domination, banner keeps or an end date, with a victory clock
- **Comfort:** push notifications while the game is closed, five colour themes in light and dark plus a black mode, medieval or classic icons, a live map with movement lines, a tutorial for new players, the Now list
- **For hosts:** one Docker image, a guided setup, the admin page and the `bannerfall-admin` console, signed releases, update and backup scripts, several worlds on one server, and an optional login through an account server shared by several worlds

## What is new

**A new look: the land and the sky above it**
- Every page lies on the land, with lists and values standing on it, parted by thin lines instead of cards and boxes. The header is the sky over the hills: the sun by day, the moon and stars at night. One typeface (Bricolage Grotesque) for everything
- **Five colour themes**, each for day and night, and **Black** for OLED screens: Meadow (the default), Cornfield, Frost, Heath and Stitched, a tapestry look with a woven ground, dashed lines, a seam in the main button and a title typeface of its own. The village picture and the map take on the theme. Old choices move along: Royal blue becomes Frost, Purple becomes Heath, Leather and forest becomes Cornfield, every other one becomes Meadow
- **One main button per place**, in the action colour; every other button is quiet. Red stays for war: attacks, losses, cancelling and deleting
- **On a phone** the sky shrinks to a slim strip with the village and the resources once you scroll, so more of the page stays in view. **On a computer** the menu is a rail on the left, with the chat as an entry of its own, and the village switch is a small menu under the village's name
- Facts are plain coloured words instead of pills, rows have thin lines instead of boxes, links that open another page end in "›", and "Block" sits behind the "…" next to a player's profile
- The pages before the game (login, registration, joining, the host's setup) and the account server's pages wear the same look

**Now: everything that runs, in one list**
- One list of builds, training, dismissals, research, protection, marches (out and in) and merchants, with the cancel and return buttons. A switch chooses "This village" or "All villages" (remembered per browser). Attacks on the way in show for every village whatever the switch says, and name the village they are going to
- From 1600 px wide it is a **column** at the right edge, with the chat under it, and the village page and the rally point drop the cards it replaces. From 1024 px it is a **tab** at the right edge (with a count, and a red dot while an attack is coming) that opens a drawer; on the map it is the same at every width. On **phones** you pull the sky down to open it; the attack alarm and a "Now" tile in the More menu open it too

**Smaller changes**
- Town Hall rows keep name, costs and button together at every width; the German recruit tabs are no longer cut off; scroll bars inside panes are thin; the chat window no longer covers the Now tab; small controls and links have a 44 px touch target
- The phone tab bar never cuts a label ("Sammelplatz" stays whole at 320 px), six-digit stocks no longer widen the header, the chat's "Minimise" hands the focus back to the menu, and the admin tabs scroll to the open one
- The service worker that shows push notifications is 16 kB instead of 262 kB
- More tests: pages such as the Town Hall, the guilds, the admin page, the market, the warehouse and player profiles now have render tests in both languages

## For hosts

- **One database migration**, run by Flyway on the first start: V67 takes the cascading delete off `research_order.village_id` (a village can no longer be deleted while research rows name it; nothing in the game deletes villages). It changes a constraint only, no data, and takes moments
- **Signed releases.** Every release tag from 2.0.0 on is signed with the release key, and its message names the server image by digest. `ops/bannerfall-update` (and so the auto-update) checks the signature against `ops/release-signers` **of the version that runs now**, and pulls the image by that digest. Nothing of an unverified release is checked out, pulled or run. Needs `git` and `ssh-keygen` on the server (`sudo apt install openssh-client`). Details in the README (*Signed releases*) and `docs/hosting.md` (section 10)

  - **Fingerprint:** `SHA256:JygsMAhABKImRGxm9kGuwRl0CZcniRg7vfGBib6tIqM` (check it against the line in `ops/release-signers` and against the public repository's release page)
  - **The image digest** of this release is on the GitHub Release page, below the text, and in the tag's message
- **Wrong passwords:** after 20 wrong passwords in 30 minutes for one account, over all addresses, further logins of that name (and password checks for changing it) are refused until the half hour is over, or the host resets the password. Open sessions go on. The account server has the same budget per account for its login form (`accounts.limits.account-failures` and `account-window-minutes`). A trade-off to know: whoever knows a name can keep its owner from logging in anew for half an hour
- **Setup code:** it ends after 5 wrong claims; `bannerfall-admin setup` makes a new one
- **Optional encrypted off-site backup:** `ops/bannerfall-backup` can copy each dump to somewhere else, encrypted with `age`, when `BACKUP_AGE_RECIPIENT` (the public key) and `BACKUP_OFFSITE_CMD` are set. It never sends anything unencrypted; a failed copy exits 1 and keeps the local dump
- **API:** new `GET /api/now`; nothing was removed. The text of the error `TOO_MANY_LOGIN_ATTEMPTS` no longer says "a minute"
- **Account server:** restyled and with the per-account budget; rebuild its image when you run one (optional for the game itself)
- **Nothing else to configure:** no new required environment variables, no change to Caddy, `compose.prod.yaml` or `edge/`

## Upgrading a running world (from v1.7.x)

1. `cd /opt/bannerfall && git fetch --tags origin`
2. **Check the signature once by hand** (the update script of 1.7.x does not check): put the key line above after `bannerfall-releases ` in a file, e.g. `echo 'bannerfall-releases namespaces="git" ssh-ed25519 AAAAC3NzaC1lZDI1NTE5AAAAIH7TeXAugUvqmHrDTY3mf4mk1dU6hEuGW3vUbFYC6blc' > /tmp/signers`, then `git -c gpg.format=ssh -c gpg.ssh.allowedSignersFile=/tmp/signers verify-tag v2.0.0`. It must print `Good "git" signature`. From 2.0.0 on the update script does this for every release
3. `ops/bannerfall-update 2.0.0` (it checks the tag out itself; add `--build` if you build your images on the server). It takes the backup, warns the players with a popup, restarts a minute later, applies V67 and checks the new version (schema 67)
4. Several worlds behind the shared Caddy: update every world, then check out the same tag in the edge folder (`edge/` itself is unchanged)

Or by hand, as before: announce the update (`bannerfall-admin broadcast "Update in 5 minutes"`), take a backup (`ops/bannerfall-backup`), pull the image below or build it with `--build-arg VERSION=2.0.0`, set `BANNERFALL_IMAGE` in `.env` to it, `docker compose -f compose.prod.yaml up -d`, then check `bannerfall-admin status` (version 2.0.0) and `bannerfall-admin check`.

Going back to 1.7.1 means restoring the backup taken before the update and starting the old image: the migration can't be undone, and the play since the update is lost.
